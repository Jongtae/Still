Once a day, while the owner is away, read the owner's recent requests one by one and find what they said about themselves that the assistant should keep. This is the first of two passes; the second tidies what is remembered, so here only find. turns lists the requests finished since the last look back, oldest first: each line starts with its ref in brackets and the request's local time, then the owner's own words (owner:) and what the assistant answered (assistant:, model-stated and never by itself a fact about the owner). owner_profile is what is remembered now: each line starts with its id in brackets. clock is now.

Read every turn, not only the long ones. Look for what is easy to miss because it was said in passing: how the owner lives and gets around, what they own, use or have tried, what they liked or will not have again, places and services they rely on and how, plans and obligations with their dates, corrections of what the assistant believed, and what they asked for again on separate occasions. proposals are those facts when owner_profile does not already hold them, even in other words.

Use kind "stated" for what the owner said about themselves, in the owner's own words, and kind "inferred" for a reasonable inference from what the owner said. category is one of: {categories}. memory_key starts with "profile." and names the fact (profile.<category>.<name>); to correct a key already in owner_profile use that key and set supersedes_key to that key (a key, never an id), otherwise supersedes_key is an empty string. invalidates is empty in this pass. Write dates as absolute dates from clock; when a fact holds only until a known day set valid_until to that day as YYYY-MM-DD, otherwise an empty string. content and evidence are at most 200 characters each; evidence is a short quote or paraphrase of what the owner said. Write into content the situation a preference or habit holds for (when buying groceries, for golf gear, on weekday mornings), so it is not applied where it does not belong. sources lists the refs of the turns a proposal comes from: at least one, and for an inferred fact at least two separate turns, because a pattern is never drawn from one occasion.

A fact is never a request: what the owner asked the assistant to do, find or watch, and what they were deciding in it, is not a fact about them. A condition the owner limited to one request (this time, for this request) stays with that request. A quantity that holds only at one moment (a balance, an amount still owed, a price) is kept only with the date it held. Where the owner is right now, what they are doing now and other current states are the day's record, not lasting facts. Keep the strength of what the owner said: a passing reaction to one occasion is about that occasion. How the software connects, signs in, asks for approval or waits for the owner is the software's own process, never a fact about the owner. Never propose health, finances, relationships, beliefs, credentials or anything about other people unless the owner stated it about themselves for a purpose (then kind "stated"). Propose at most {gather_limit}, and an empty list when nothing new was said.

This judgment writes nothing.
