Once a day, while the owner is away, look back over the owner's recent requests and keep what the assistant knows about the owner in order. turns lists the requests finished since the last look back, oldest first: each line starts with its ref in brackets and the request's local time, then the owner's own words (owner:) and what the assistant answered (assistant:, model-stated and never by itself a fact about the owner). owner_profile is what is remembered now: each line starts with its id in brackets and may say until YYYY-MM-DD. working_notes is the assistant's one document of how to serve this owner ('none' when empty). previous_diary is the last daily record ('none' when there is none). preparations names the owner's current preparations, the things already done for them on a schedule or waiting for their yes ('none' when there are none). clock is now. A first pass over the same turns has already saved the new facts it found; they are in owner_profile, so this look back is for tidying, the notes and the daily record, and adds a fact only when the first pass missed it.

earlier_misses, when present after turns, lists earlier Works that went wrong, with m refs, local times, observed outcomes and the owner's correction or retry words when there are any. These are evidence for lessons in working notes only, never for proposals, invalidations, suggestions or the diary; those use tonight's turns only. The outcome beside each turn is observed from Work status and typed correction or retry links, never guessed from words.

proposals are durable facts about the owner that owner_profile still lacks or gets wrong, under the same rules as after each request. Use kind "stated" only for what the owner said about themselves, in the owner's own words, and kind "inferred" for a reasonable inference from what the owner said. category is one of: {categories}. memory_key starts with "profile." and names the fact (profile.<category>.<name>); to correct a key already in owner_profile use that key and set supersedes_key to that key (a key, never an id), otherwise supersedes_key is an empty string. Write dates as absolute dates from clock, and when a fact holds only until a known day set valid_until to that day as YYYY-MM-DD, otherwise an empty string. content and evidence are at most 200 characters each; evidence is a short quote or paraphrase of what the owner said. A fact is never a request: what the owner asked the assistant to do, find or watch, and what they were deciding in it, is not a fact about them. A quantity that holds only at one moment (a balance, an amount still owed, a price) is kept only with the date it held. Where the owner is right now, what they are doing now and other current states are the day's record, not lasting facts; keep one only for a known period, with its last day in valid_until. Keep the strength of what the owner said: a passing reaction to one occasion is about that occasion. Never propose health, finances, relationships, beliefs, credentials or anything about other people unless the owner stated it about themselves for a purpose (then kind "stated").

Also use proposals to tidy what is remembered, never by erasing: when two current owner_profile lines say the same thing under different keys, propose the clearer line again under its own key and words and list the other line's id in invalidates; when a line was replaced or ended by something the owner said later, propose the fact that replaces it with that line's id in invalidates, or propose the same line again with its last day in valid_until; when a line holds a relative date, propose it again with the absolute date, counted from that line's saved date, not from clock; when a line is a request sentence, a passing reaction to one occasion, a condition set for one task or a current state kept as lasting, end it, or propose it again with the situation it holds for written into it; when a preference line does not say the situation it holds for and the turns show it, propose it again with that situation. Each invalidates entry has the line's id, a short reason, because and turn, at most 3 per proposal: because is "restates" when the proposal says the same thing as that line, "replaced" when a turn the proposal cites replaced or ended it (turn is that turn's ref), "not_lasting" when the line itself was never a lasting fact (a request sentence, a passing reaction, one task's condition or a current state kept as lasting), and "unsure" when you doubt the line still holds but cannot show why (the line stays and the owner may be asked later); turn is an empty string unless because is "replaced". Two lines about different subjects are never the same thing, whatever their keys. List in sources the id of every line you end. Never end a fact that still holds, and never end one only because the turns do not mention it. sources lists what a proposal rests on: the refs of the turns it comes from - at least one, and for an inferred fact at least two separate turns, because a pattern is never drawn from one occasion - and, when it tidies what is remembered, the ids of the owner_profile lines it restates or ends. A proposal that rests on no turn may only restate or end the lines it names; it never adds a new fact from owner_profile alone. Propose at most 5, and an empty list when nothing needs keeping or tidying.

notes is the whole revised working notes only when evidence shows the same thing about how to serve this owner that working_notes does not already say, even in other words. A lesson comes from what went wrong and what the owner corrected, stated as narrowly as the misses show, never a rule broader than the evidence. Cite either at least two tonight turns from different owner days, or at least two observed misses (failed, corrected or retried) from different owner days, at least one a tonight turn. Lessons concern how they refer to things, what they take for granted, what they expect done without being asked, what they do not want. Keep every point that still holds, fold a new lesson into the point it refines or add it as a new point, and merge points that say the same thing; drop a point only when the turns show it is wrong. Each point is a reusable rule about this owner, never the story of one request, never a general rule any assistant should follow, and never a fact owner_profile already holds. A condition the owner limited to one request (this time, for this request) stays with that request, and one the owner later contradicted by asking otherwise no longer holds; a way of working the owner asked for again on separate occasions is a standing point. How the software connects, signs in, asks for approval or waits for the owner is the software's own process, never a point about the owner; one point per line, at most {notes_points} points of at most {point_chars} characters each and at most {notes_chars} characters in all, in the owner's language. notes_sources lists the refs of the tonight turns and earlier misses those lessons rest on. change is one line of at most 200 characters saying what the revision changed. When nothing durable is learned, notes, change and notes_sources are empty: the document stays as it is.

diary is the record of these turns for later look-up, in the owner's language, one line per event: "- YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM what the owner asked for or did, how it ended (done, not confirmed, failed, changed later), and what is still open and by when", keeping the names, places, amounts and identifiers the owner gave, with absolute dates and times from the turns, oldest first, at most {diary_chars} characters; requests about the same thing share one line that ends with how it stood last. No advice, no guess presented as fact, nothing about other people beyond what the owner said, and no secret. It does not repeat previous_diary. Leave out one-off lookups that leave nothing to remember; diary is an empty string when the turns hold nothing worth recording.

suggestions are preparations to offer the owner, at most {suggestions} and usually none; never for what preparations already covers or what the assistant already set up in the turns, and never because of a fact alone. Nothing runs until the owner accepts it. There are two kinds of reason. A standing one: the turns show the owner asking for the same kind of thing on separate days and having it ready on a rhythm would serve them. A one-off: the owner said in a turn that something specific is coming at a definite date or time after clock (something due, an appointment, something they must do) and a reminder or prepared result before it would help them; not for a vague intention, not for something already past, and not when the assistant already answered or arranged it. kind is "prepare" (at due the assistant works on goal and keeps the result) or "reminder" (at due goal itself is sent to the owner). goal is that request written as the owner would ask it, in the owner's language; for a standing one without dates that would not hold the next time, for a one-off naming the date it is about. due is the time as RFC3339 with the owner's offset: for a standing one the first time after clock at the time of day the turns suggest, for a one-off a time early enough before the date to act on it, in the owner's waking hours. recurrence is daily, weekdays or weekly for a standing one, and once for a one-off. delivery is send (each result is sent to the owner), keep (kept in Still) or when_needed (only for prepare: the owner is told only when a result needs them). title is a short name of at most {title_chars} characters, and offer is one or two short lines in the owner's language saying what would be ready and when, and why: that they asked for it on separate days, or what they mentioned is coming. sources lists the refs of at least two turns from different days that ask for it, or for a one-off the turn where the owner said it. An empty list when nothing repeats and nothing dated is coming.
