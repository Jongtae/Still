What is confirmed and open questions

This page separates what the two manuscripts confirmed from what they did not. It sets out Still's evidence boundary, the verification plan not yet carried out, and the open questions.

Manuscripts: Whitepaper ch. 12–13, Compendium ch. 22–24. Last updated October 14, 2026.

A case, not a proof

Basis Observed. Whitepaper ch. 12, Compendium 22.1

The two manuscripts do not treat Still as a finished product that proves the industry's future. They treat it as a development case that makes the industry's questions concrete and draws out what needs verifying.

The real-use research of September 23, 2026 showed two things. Having an internal structure that manages task execution is separate from the user feeling they work with a persistent counterpart. Having a selection screen is also different from that selection actually taking effect.

One project's observations do not prove how often something happens across the industry, but they can show concretely what the industry needs to verify.

What is not claimed today

Basis Contract. Compendium 22.2, product status document

The product status document makes no current claim for the following.

  • Autonomous purchasing
  • Arbitrary computer use
  • Universal web verification
  • Live stock and checkout verification
  • Arbitrary third-party package execution
  • A public marketplace

Some things have also not yet been observed: the decision model actually running on the owner's accounts, the cause of the mismatch between real Telegram behavior and the automated tests' expectations, how well a model follows a skill's method on real sites, and actual continuation, through to the end, of a service request that was not prepared in advance.

The verification agenda

Basis Proposal. Whitepaper ch. 13

If it stops at listing principles that are easy to agree with, this direction will not become an empirical claim. It needs experiments that actually compare. The items the whitepaper proposes:

Note This table is not a table of results from experiments performed. Participants, repetitions, budget and prior criteria are not yet set.

The whitepaper also sets out evaluation conditions. It does not pick only successes: it includes failures during model switches, stale memories creeping in, expired authority, users changing course midway, and external effects that could not be confirmed. The criteria are set before the experiment, not after seeing results. A well-working integrated product is included as a comparison.

The whitepaper states that if experiments show the benefit of transfer is small, or the complexity and cost of an independent agent layer keep exceeding the benefit, the product strategy must change.

Open questions

Source Compendium ch. 24

Authority and protection

  • Can transparency replace prevention? The information-use audit tells you what happened but does not stop it, and injection and exfiltration defences are parked.
  • How temporary is the pilot? Pilot exceptions are layered over the base rules of several contracts, and the hardening program is not yet defined.
  • How does a mandate connect to existing Grants? Integrating it into actual behavior is deferred.

Judgment and orchestration

  • Mid-work corrections do not reach the decision model, so re-delegation can happen on a stale plan.
  • Restricting delegation to the main AI's family narrows the original idea considerably, and the recorded basis is a single owner decision.
  • Codex and Claude Code differ in how they handle outside connections and skills, so they are not controlled to the same degree.

Memory

  • How confidence should be expressed is not yet settled.
  • The three altitudes of forgetting do not yet mesh.
  • Today's memory store records no validity period or retraction time.

Skills and connections

  • There is not yet a mechanism for an AI to safely write a new skill for the owner.
  • The safest route may be the least capable. On connections where a step cannot be checked midway, changes are not offered at all, so real work is pushed onto other routes.
  • Whether the premise of using a service company's own official skill first actually holds has not been confirmed.

Product

  • Local first is not local only. Subscription AIs run on provider servers.
  • Truthfulness and apparent usefulness sometimes conflict. The rule of never saying "delivered" without a receipt can end many shares as "delivery uncertain".
  • Features judged finished in development have several times behaved differently in real use. Ways to narrow the distance between the test environment and real use are still being refined.
