# Summary or analysis

Use this before you work through material for the owner, when it is not plain which kind of answer they want. The kind of answer decides what you do and when you are finished; the topic does not.

Do not use it for a single fact lookup or for an action (a change, a booking, a message). Those have their own finish line.

## 1. Read the requested form

Take the form from the owner's own words and the conversation, in this order:

1. A form they named: "요약해줘", "summarize", "compare", "what do you think", "should I", "is this right".
2. What they will do with the answer, when they said it: decide, reply to someone, check a fact, catch up.
3. If neither settles it, give a short summary and offer the analysis in one line. Do not ask first.

The forms differ like this:

| Form | What it does | Your own judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summary | Restates what the material says, faithfully and shorter | None, except what to leave out |
| Comparison | Lines up two or more things on the points that matter to the owner | Which points matter; no winner unless asked |
| Analysis | Tests the claims: support, counter-evidence, disagreement, uncertainty | Yes, and each judgment shows its reason |
| Recommendation | Picks an option for the owner's situation | Yes, with the reason and what would change it |

## 2. Do only that form

- A summary keeps the author's claims as the author's. Write "the article says", not "it is true that". Do not add verdicts, and do not drop a point because you disagree with it.
- A comparison uses the same points for every option and says where a point could not be found for one of them.
- An analysis follows the `claim-analysis` method when it is installed: claims, support, counter-evidence, uncertainty, a justified conclusion.
- A recommendation names the option and the one or two reasons that decide it for this owner, and what would make another option better.

When the owner asked for one form and a different one would clearly help more, give what they asked for first, then offer the other in one line.

## 3. When the answer is finished

A finished answer is judged by its form, never by its length or by a fixed number of points.

- **Summary:** every main point of the material is there, in proportion, with nothing the material does not say. A long document may need more points and a short one fewer; numbers such as "three to five points" are a starting guess, not a rule.
- **Comparison:** every option is covered on every point, and missing information is named.
- **Analysis:** each claim you assessed has its support and its weak spots stated, and the conclusion follows from them. Claims you could not check are named as unchecked.
- **Recommendation:** the choice, its reasons and its conditions are stated, and the facts it rests on are sourced.

If you cannot finish the form (the material is missing, a source would not open), say which part is missing instead of filling it with a different form.
