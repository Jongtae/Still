# Cross-checking a source

Use this when an answer depends on a fact, a number or a quotation that you have from one place so far, and getting it wrong would mislead the owner. It applies to any subject.

Do not use it for a fact the owner gave you about themselves, or for a figure a connected service returned about the owner's own account. Those are the record.

## 1. Find where the fact comes from

- Follow the fact back to its origin: the agency, company, study, court record or person that first published it. A news article or a summary page that repeats it is not the origin.
- Read the origin itself when you can open it, and note its date.
- If you cannot find the origin, say the fact is reported by the source you have, not that it is established.

## 2. Look for independent confirmation

- Two pages that copy the same press release or the same article are one source, not two. Sources are independent when they reached the fact separately.
- Prefer, in order: the origin; a source with its own access to the facts (a regulator, an official dataset, a party to the event); a reputable outlet that names its own source.
- One good independent confirmation is usually enough. Stop when the answer is settled; do not keep searching for its own sake.

## 3. Compare carefully

Before calling two sources in conflict, check that they mean the same thing:

- **Date:** which period or moment each figure describes, and when each source was published or updated.
- **Definition:** what exactly is counted (gross or net, with or without tax, a region or the whole country).
- **Units and rounding:** currency, per-person or total, rounded or exact.

Many apparent conflicts are a different date or definition. Say which one it is.

## 4. Report what you found

Say one of these plainly, with the source links next to the fact:

- **Confirmed:** the origin and an independent source agree; give the figure and its date.
- **Single source:** only one source has it; give it as that source's claim.
- **Conflicting:** sources disagree after the checks in step 3; give each figure with its source and date, say which you consider more reliable and why, and do not average them.
- **Unverifiable:** you could not open or find a source; say what you tried.

Keep exact numbers, currencies, dates and quotations as the source gives them.
